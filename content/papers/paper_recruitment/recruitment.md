##### Abstract:

This paper studies how firms alter their recruitment behaviour when faced with changes in scarcity of labour. I exploit access to micro data from a recruitment survey in Denmark to examine the impacts on firms recruitment policies, such as using additional search channels and changing hiring standards. I construct detailed submarket measures of the relevant competition a firm faces relative to the supply of labour. To address endogeneity issues, I exploit variation arising from mass layoff events in other industries, combined with a fixed effects design.  I find that in tighter markets firms respond by using more search channels and offering higher wages. I find limited evidence on hiring standards -- but suggestively negative effects. I extend the analysis  to the firm level in a shift share IV setting. I find evidence that internal adjustments in the firm, specifically through internal recruitment and retention, are important forms of adjustment to hiring difficulties, in addition to increased intensity in the external market.
