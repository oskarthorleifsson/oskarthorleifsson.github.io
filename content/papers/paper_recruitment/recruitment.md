##### Abstract:

This paper studies how firms alter their recruitment behavior when labor market conditions tighten. I combine microdata from a recruitment survey in Denmark -- which contains direct information on the recruitment process for specific vacancies -- with administrative data to examine how firms adjust their recruitment behavior, including hiring standards, the use of alternative recruitment channels, starting wages, and the characteristics of new hires. I construct detailed submarket measures of the competition firms face for workers relative to the available labor supply. For identification, I exploit variation in labor market tightness arising from mass layoff events in other industries, combined with a fixed-effects design. I find that when labor markets tighten, firms are more likely to use alternative recruitment channels and hire workers with less work experience. Starting wages also increase, but modestly, with an estimated elasticity of around 0.07. I extend the analysis from vacancy-level to firm-level outcomes using a shift-share IV design. I find evidence of increased retention of incumbents and suggestive evidence of greater internal hiring when labor markets tighten. Overall, the findings show that firms respond to the scarcity of suitable candidates along multiple margins.
