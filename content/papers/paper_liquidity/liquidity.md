##### Abstract:
Using Danish administrative data on individual balance sheets and job applications, we study how financial resources available at unemployment entry affect job search behavior and labor market outcomes. By observing actual job applications, we examine how workers allocate search across labor market opportunities. We find that workers with fewer financial resources experience shorter unemployment spells but subsequently earn lower wages and enter less stable employment relationships. During unemployment, these workers systematically target lower-paying jobs, jobs with shorter expected duration, and jobs requiring longer commutes. The application responses reveal dimensions of job search behavior that are difficult to infer from realized labor market outcomes alone. Our results suggest that financial resources affect not only reemployment timing but also the types of jobs workers pursue and the trade-offs they make during search.
